Transparency

Methodology & sourcing

Every modeled figure on this page is built from a stated formula applied uniformly across all 24 trims — nothing is fit or adjusted per vehicle. Raw specs (range, battery, charging, cargo, warranty) come from manufacturer data and named publications; where a figure couldn't be confirmed, the cell reads "—" instead of a guess.

Qualification threshold
A trim is included if its manufacturer-stated base MSRP, before destination fee, is under $45,000. Destination fees run roughly $1,395–$2,045 across these models and are not included in the price shown, for an apples-to-apples comparison of the number manufacturers actually advertise as "starting at."
Est. VA price
Trim MSRP × 1.0415 (Virginia's 4.15% Motor Vehicle Sales and Use Tax, $75 minimum). This is a tax-inclusive sticker estimate, not a record of what anyone actually paid — no public dataset of real Virginia EV transaction prices exists. It does not include destination, title/registration fees, dealer add-ons, or any discount off MSRP. The federal $7,500 credit is not applied because it expired for deliveries after September 30, 2025; Virginia has no funded statewide EV purchase rebate operating in 2026.
Efficiency score (1–10)
Built from EPA combined MPGe. Where MPGe wasn't published for a trim, an equivalent is derived as (EPA range ÷ battery kWh) × 33.7. Every trim's effective MPGe is then min–max normalized across all 24 trims to a 1–10 scale. A trim with neither MPGe nor both range and battery kWh confirmed shows "—".
Charging & battery score (1–10)
A weighted blend of two normalized (min–max, 1–10) components across all 24 trims: 60% peak DC fast-charge rate (kW) and 40% battery capacity (kWh, as reported — gross for some models, usable for others, since manufacturers don't disclose consistently). If only one component is confirmed for a trim, that component is used at full weight; if neither is confirmed, the cell shows "—".
0–30 / 0–45 mph estimates
Derived from each trim's published (or closely attributed) 0–60 mph time using ratios drawn from instrumented EV tests: 0–30 ≈ 38% of the 0–60 time; 0–45 ≈ 65% of the 0–60 time. This reflects the fact that most EVs post their fastest acceleration low in the range and taper as power limits kick in near highway speed — it is a modeled estimate, not a test result, and trims with no confirmed 0–60 show "—" for both.
Est. 5-year upkeep
5 × [(3,159 × trim MSRP ÷ 45,000) + 540]. The $3,159 figure is Insurify's reported 2026 average annual full-coverage insurance cost for EVs generally; it's scaled here to each trim's price relative to this page's $45,000 ceiling, since insurance cost correlates with vehicle value. The $540 figure is 12,000 miles/year × $0.045/mile, near the low end of Consumer Reports' reported EV maintenance range (roughly 3–6¢/mile, versus 6–10¢/mile for gas vehicles). This estimate excludes electricity cost, tires, purchase price, financing, and any model-specific insurance data, since no source breaks insurance cost out by this specific set of models.
Warranty format
Shown as basic bumper-to-bumper / powertrain / battery, in years and thousands of miles (e.g., "3/36" = 3 years or 36,000 miles). Some manufacturers combine powertrain and battery coverage into one term; shown as stated by the manufacturer. Warranty cells are identical on the Used '25 tab because they show the factory term as new — a 2025 example with under 10,000 miles has typically used about a year and 10,000 miles of it, so real remaining coverage is slightly less than shown (e.g., a 3-year/36,000-mile basic warranty has roughly 2 years/26,000 miles left).
Used '25 tab methodology
Each row is that model's cheapest 2026 trim, priced instead as a typical 2025-model-year, sub-10,000-mile used example. "Est. VA used price" is that typical private-party value (per KBB where available, otherwise a nationwide average asking-listing price) plus Virginia's 4.15% sales tax — real used-market data, unlike the new-car tab's tax-on-MSRP estimate, but still a typical value rather than a specific transaction. All other columns (efficiency, charging, cargo, acceleration, comfort) reuse that trim's engineering specs, since those don't change between the 2025 and 2026 versions of the same generation — with one exception: the Tesla Model Y didn't offer a "Standard" trim in 2025, so that row is priced and labeled as a 2025 Long Range example while still showing the 2026 Standard trim's specs as the closest available approximation. Two models — Subaru Uncharted and the crossover-bodied Nissan Leaf — are both new for 2026 with no qualifying 2025 predecessor and are left off this tab entirely.
Low used-availability flag
A row is flagged when 2025 U.S. sales volume was unusually low relative to the rest of this fleet, meaning a real search for a sub-10,000-mile 2025 example will likely turn up far fewer listings even though the typical price qualifies. The Volvo EX30 sold 5,409 U.S. units in 2025 — its first American model year — and Volvo has since announced it's ending U.S. EX30 sales, so that population is fixed and won't grow. The VinFast VF8 is flagged even harder: VinFast doesn't publish a clean full-year, model-specific U.S. sales figure, but reported registrations show just 797 VF8s in the U.S. in the first half of 2025 alone — an order of magnitude below every other model here. The Kia Niro EV's exact 2025 EV-only volume is also unclear (Kia's own reporting combines Niro hybrid, plug-in hybrid, and EV sales into one number), but nothing found suggests a scarcity on the scale of the EX30 or VF8, so it isn't flagged.
Why the Hyundai Kona Electric isn't included
Hyundai paused U.S. production of the Kona Electric for model year 2026 after a 41% year-over-year sales drop following the federal credit's expiration; dealers are selling remaining 2025-MY inventory as new. As of this September 2026 update, Hyundai's plan is to resume production as a 2027 model (SE trim only, ~200-mile range, smaller 48.6kWh battery), targeted to restart around mid-2026 — but no confirmed dealer on-sale date has been found yet. Since this page compares "the newest models," it stays excluded rather than shown against a stale model year — the 2025 Kona Electric started at $32,875 (SE) if you want to compare it yourself.
September 2026 update notes
Added: Chevrolet Equinox EV. A gap in the original research pass — it's sold in the US for MY2026, is crossover-bodied, and all three trims (LT1 $34,995, LT2 $41,795, RS $44,095) clear the $45,000 threshold. It was the top-selling non-Tesla EV in the US for 2025 (57,945 units), so it isn't low-availability-flagged on the Used '25 tab. Only FWD pricing is shown; AWD variants exist at added cost but weren't sourced this pass.
Kia Niro EV — last model year notice: multiple sources now report MY2026 will be the Niro EV's final model year in the US. Pricing and specs on this page are unchanged, but expect it to disappear from future updates once Kia confirms an end date.
VinFast VF8 cargo correction: the Eco trim's cargo figure was corrected from 48.4 to 13.2 cu ft — 48.4 is the seats-folded maximum, not the "behind rear seats" figure this column tracks for every other row. Both Eco and the newly added Plus trim now show 13.2 cu ft for consistency.
Other models considered and excluded
Over the $45,000 threshold: Volkswagen ID.4 (base now $45,095), Chevrolet Blazer EV ($46,495), Honda Prologue ($47,400), Cadillac Optiq (~$50,900), Genesis GV60 (~$52,525), Buick Electra E5 (exactly $45,000, not under it), Polestar 4 ($56,300 — not sold at the $46,400 figure once quoted for it elsewhere, corrected here), Jeep Wagoneer S ($67,995) and Recon ($65,000), and Rivian R2 (cheapest available trim is Premium at $53,990; the promised ~$45k Standard RWD trim doesn't arrive until early 2027). The VinFast VF8 Plus dropped to $44,900 as of a May 2026 price cut and now qualifies — it's included above as of this update.
How close did excluded models come?
On seating (non-crossover candidates): the BMW i4 is the one genuinely borderline case — reviewers describe its seat height as sitting between a sports sedan and an SUV, with ground clearance (4.9 in) higher than a typical low sedan but still short of any crossover on this page. It doesn't clear the price bar either way, though: new base is $52,800, and even sub-10,000-mile 2025 used examples mostly run $44,000–$67,000, with only rare outliers dipping toward $44,000. Everything else checked resolved cleanly rather than sitting in between: the Hyundai Kona Electric and Kia Niro EV are both crossover-classified despite compact proportions (not non-crossover candidates at all), the VW ID.3 and ID.7 were never brought to the US, and no electric wagon currently sells here under $45,000.
On price (crossovers excluded for exceeding $45,000 new): most would in fact qualify used. A typical sub-10,000-mile 2025 example runs roughly $32,600 for the VW ID.4, $34,000 for the Chevrolet Blazer EV, $30,000 for the Honda Prologue, and $29,400–$40,000 for the Genesis GV60 — all comfortably under $45,000 used despite failing the new-car cutoff. The Cadillac Optiq is a closer call: typical low-mileage 2025 examples run about $42,000–$44,200, just under the line. The Polestar 4 mostly stays over $45,000 even used ($44,200–$51,400). The Buick Electra E5 can't be evaluated at all — like the Subaru Uncharted and the redesigned Nissan Leaf, it simply isn't sold in the US, new or used, as of this writing. None of these appear on the Used '25 tab above; this is reporting what a wider search turned up, not an endorsement to add them without further review.
On price, at the high end of already-included trims: the Ford Mustang Mach-E GT is worth a specific flag — very-low-mileage 2025 examples were found at $45,977 and $46,590, right at or just over the used cutoff, even though the Select/Premium trims shown in this comparison stay well under $45,000 both new and used. Higher trims of the Kia EV6, Hyundai Ioniq 5, and Tesla Model Y all stayed comfortably under $45,000 used even at their priciest configurations.
Not currently sold new in the US: Nissan Ariya (skipping US MY2026), Fiat 600e (never sold in the US), Rivian R2 (cheapest trim doesn't arrive until late 2027), VinFast VF6/VF7 (announced, then pulled from VinFast's North America plans), Mazda MX-30 (discontinued for the US after MY2023), Fisker Ocean (Fisker ceased operations via bankruptcy in 2024), and smart, MG, BYD, Xpeng, and Zeekr (none sold in the US retail market).
Wrong body style: the Polestar 2 is a hatchback/fastback, not a crossover or SUV, regardless of price.
Not yet on sale: Scout Motors' Traveler, expected in 2027 at a substantially higher price point.
